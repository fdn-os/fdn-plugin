# Aligned scope and outcomes format

Contract for **this build run**. Not the long-lived design of the artifact. That UI is out of scope.

Four parts, these names, this order:

1. **Goal of this build run** — why this run exists; what is different when it is done
2. **User journeys** — numbered, in the person's words (`when you …, you can …`), **Primary** marked. Not plan steps (no steps, checks, or environment)
3. **What we will do** — in-scope outcomes for this run. Checkable. Not the plan (no steps, checks, or environment)
4. **What we will not do** — limits for this run, including every "we will not do this now" that was grilled out of a parked or leftover finding. Do not hang those as "saved for later."

Omit empty only if a part would be a lie. Do not invent a fifth section. Do not pour the plan into "What we will do."

A **key** is any checkable commitment in those four parts. A journey is a key. Challenge, Plan, and accept use that meaning.

On a change to existing work: Goal = what will be different; User journeys = what they can do once it is done; What we will do = the change; What we will not do = must-not-change.

Human approve is the four parts and the short approve banner (`surfaces/ad-approve.txt`): bullets, bold on the words that matter, key points, not a dump.

## Authority

Conflict with the four parts → stop and reopen Align. The plan derives from the sealed aligned scope and outcomes. An already-decided file may inform; it does not outrank the sealed four parts.
